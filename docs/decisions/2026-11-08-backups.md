# X3. Backups and restore: plan and runbook

Card P1-09, PRD X3. Written 2026-11-08 by the W2-7 package. Status: plan approved by the master (2026-11-08). Turning anything on in Railway needs Erick's yes.

## What Railway offers (checked in Railway's docs, 2026-11-08, read only)

- A service with a volume can have backups made by hand or on a schedule: daily, weekly or monthly.
- Daily backups are kept 6 days. Weekly backups are kept 27 days. Monthly backups are kept 89 days.
- A restore makes a fresh volume from the **whole** backup and mounts it. The old volume stays in the project, unmounted.
- Wiping a volume deletes all of its backups.
- The Railway command line can list, download and upload files on a volume (`railway volume files`).

So a Railway restore is all or nothing. Restoring one student that way would roll every student back to the backup's time. That is why there is a second layer below.

## The plan

| What | How | How often | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| The whole volume (`tenants/`, `auth/`, `library/`, `metering/`, `backups/`) | Railway scheduled backups | Daily and weekly. No monthly. | Daily 6 days, weekly 27 days |
| Each tenant folder, on its own | The front door copies it to `<volume>/backups/nightly/<date>/<tenant id>/`, with a manifest of every file's size and SHA-256 | Once a night, at the first hourly job tick after 10:00 UTC (2 or 3 a.m. Pacific) | The newest 7 nights |
| The database | Supabase's own daily backups (Pro plan) | Daily | Supabase's terms |

Why no monthly Railway backup: on day 91 a student's data is deleted (D25). A monthly backup would keep it 89 more days. With daily and weekly only, deleted data is out of every backup within 27 days. The privacy policy (T5) should say: "Deleted data leaves our backups within 30 days."

The day-91 job also deletes that tenant's nightly copies at once.

The library is not in the nightly copies. Erick has the original files, and the Railway backups hold it.

Cost: the nightly copies take about 7 times the space of the tenant folders on the volume. Tenant folders are small (chat and graph files).

## How to restore one student's folder

Use this when one student's data is damaged or lost, for example after a bad update. Other students are not touched.

1. Find the tenant id. It is on the dashboard, or in the `tenants` table.
2. Ask the student to sign out, if you can. A restore while they study can lose their last few minutes.
3. Open a shell on the Railway service (`railway ssh`, or the service's shell in the dashboard).
4. List the nights you can restore from:

   ```bash
   ls /data/backups/nightly/
   ```

5. First restore into a scratch folder and look at it. Nothing live changes:

   ```bash
   node frontdoor/backups/restore-tenant.ts --tenant <tenant id> --from /data/backups/nightly/<date> --scratch /tmp/restore-check
   ```

   It checks every file against the night's manifest and against the copy it made, and prints the file count.
6. When it is right, restore it live:

   ```bash
   node frontdoor/backups/restore-tenant.ts --tenant <tenant id> --from /data/backups/nightly/<date> --live
   ```

   - The student's current folder moves to `/data/restore-kept/<tenant id>-<time>/`. It is never deleted by the script.
   - The restored folder goes in its place.
   - The student's tutor server is stopped. The next request starts it on the restored folder.
7. Check the student's tutor works. When you are sure, remove the folder in `/data/restore-kept/`, and `/tmp/restore-check`.

The script refuses a backup that has a symlink, one that no longer matches its manifest, a tenant id that is not a UUID, and a scratch folder that is not empty.

## How to restore one student from an older Railway backup

Use this only when the 7 nightly copies are too new (the damage is older than a week).

1. In Railway, make a manual backup of the volume first.
2. Restore the chosen backup. This makes a fresh volume from it and mounts it on the service. **Every student is rolled back while it is mounted**, so do it out of hours.
3. Download the one tenant folder from it: `railway volume files download /tenants/<tenant id> ./restore/tenants/<tenant id>`.
4. Put the live data back: mount the previous volume again (Railway leaves it in the project, unmounted), or restore the backup from step 1.
5. Upload the downloaded folder to the service (`railway volume files upload`) somewhere outside `tenants/`, for example `/data/restore-in/`.
6. Run the restore script with `--from /data/restore-in` (it also finds `<folder>/tenants/<tenant id>`), first `--scratch`, then `--live`, as above.

This path has not been tried on Railway. Try it once on staging before it is needed.

## How to restore the whole volume

Use this when the volume is lost or most of it is damaged.

1. In Railway, open the project, click the front door service on the project canvas, and open its **Backups** tab.
2. Find the backup by its date stamp and click **Restore** on it. Railway makes a fresh volume from it and stages the change. Click **Details** at the top of the project canvas, check the change, and deploy it.
3. The old volume stays in the project, unmounted, until Erick removes it.
4. Any usage rows the meter was holding are on the volume too (`metering/pending-usage.jsonl`), so check the front door's start log.

## Tests

`tests/frontdoor-backups-restore.test.ts` makes a fake volume, copies it nightly, damages one tenant, then restores that tenant from the copy into a scratch folder and into the live volume. It checks every file by size and SHA-256, checks the other tenant is untouched, checks the damaged folder is kept aside, and checks a live restore stops a running tutor server for that folder. It also runs the script itself with plain Node.

## Turning it on (Erick)

Railway, for staging and again for production:

1. Open railway.com and sign in. Open the Ground Tutor project.
2. On the project canvas, click the front door service (the one with the volume mounted at `/data`).
3. In the service's settings panel, open the **Backups** tab.
4. Under the backup schedule, turn on **Daily** and **Weekly**. Leave **Monthly** off.
5. Click **Create a backup** once, so there is a backup from today.
6. Check the Backups tab the next day: a daily backup is listed.

Then:

7. Nothing to set for the nightly copies: the front door makes them once it is deployed.
8. Supabase: confirm the project's plan includes daily backups.
9. Once, on staging: try "How to restore one student's folder" with a test account.

## A secret that must never change

`LH_TRIAL_HASH_KEY` scrambles each trial email in `trial_claims`. **Never change it** once students have signed up, and keep a copy somewhere safe outside Railway. Every stored hash was made with it. A new key would let every past trial start again, and the old records could not be matched. Railway's volume backups do not hold it: it is a Railway variable, not a file.
